The Wyckoff Method is a technical analysis framework developed by Richard Wyckoff in the early 20th century. Its core insight is that price and volume tell a story about the behaviour of large institutional operators — the "Composite Man" — who accumulate and distribute positions in phases.

## The Wyckoff Cycle

Markets move through four phases in the Wyckoff framework:

1. **Accumulation** — large operators build long positions within a trading range, absorbing supply from retail sellers
2. **Mark Up** — price breaks out of the range and trends higher as demand overwhelms supply
3. **Distribution** — operators offload positions within a new range at elevated prices
4. **Mark Down** — supply overwhelms demand, price trends lower

## Key Wyckoff Events

Each phase contains identifiable structural events:

- **PS (Preliminary Support / Supply)** — first significant buying/selling that begins to slow the prior trend
- **SC / BC (Selling Climax / Buying Climax)** — high-volume reversal bar marking the end of the trend
- **AR (Automatic Rally / Reaction)** — the first impulsive move out of the climax
- **ST (Secondary Test)** — a test of the climax low/high on reduced volume, confirming the range
- **Spring / Upthrust** — a false breakout that shakes out weak hands before the real move
- **SOS / SOW (Sign of Strength / Weakness)** — the first impulse move that leaves the range
- **LPS / LPSY (Last Point of Support / Supply)** — the final pullback before the sustained trend begins

## Reading the S&P 500 Weekly Chart

### Late 2022 — Accumulation Range

The October 2022 low represented a likely Selling Climax (SC) — a sharp, high-volume capitulation that brought the index from ~4,800 down to ~3,500. The subsequent Automatic Rally (AR) into year-end established the upper boundary of the range. Multiple Secondary Tests of the 3,500–3,600 zone, each on declining volume, were consistent with absorption of remaining supply.

### The Spring — Q1 2023

A brief undercut of the trading range low in early 2023, followed by a rapid reclaim, fits the structure of a Spring. Volume dried up on the undercut and expanded on the recovery — classic Wyckoff behaviour indicating that the "Composite Man" had finished accumulating.

### Sign of Strength — Breakout and Back-Test

The expansion above the top of the range (around 4,200) on expanding volume was a clear Sign of Strength (SOS). The subsequent retest of that breakout level as new support — the Last Point of Support (LPS) — offered a high-probability re-entry.

## Current Structure Assessment

As of early 2025, the S&P 500 is showing potential Distribution characteristics in the 4,800–5,300 zone:

- Buying Climax (BC) characteristics near the 5,250–5,300 zone
- Multiple failed attempts to sustain above 5,250 on high volume
- Declining breadth — fewer stocks making new highs even as the index pushes higher
- Macro backdrop: elevated rates, credit spreads widening, leading indicators softening

> This is a working hypothesis, not a trade recommendation. Wyckoff analysis is probabilistic. Always define risk and manage position size accordingly.

## How to Use This Framework

- Identify the phase (accumulation, mark up, distribution, mark down) on the higher timeframe first
- Look for structural events (SC, AR, Spring, SOS) to confirm the phase
- Use volume as your primary confirmation tool — price tells you what, volume tells you why
- Trade in the direction of the higher-timeframe phase; fade the opposite
- Set stops below the LPS (long) or above the LPSY (short) to define risk
