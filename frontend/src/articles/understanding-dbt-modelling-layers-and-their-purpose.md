<blockquote>dbt (data build tool) is a powerful tool for transforming raw data into meaningful insights. It organizes data transformation into distinct layers, each serving a specific purpose. This structured approach ensures data is clean, consistent, and ready for analysis. Below, we delve into each layer in detail.</blockquote><h3>Landing Layer</h3><p><strong>Models — </strong>The landing layer, also called the <strong>raw zone</strong> or <strong>source layer</strong>, acts as the initial point of entry for data.</p><p><strong>Structure — </strong>1:1 reflection of source tables without any transformations.</p><p><strong>Transformations — </strong>No Transformation.</p><h3><strong>Staging Layer</strong></h3><p><strong>Models — </strong>Staging Models (<code>stg_*</code>): These models are responsible for <strong>transforming raw data</strong> from the source into a <strong>clean and consistent format</strong>. Staging layers is the replica of source with light column transformations.</p><p><strong>Structure —</strong>Typically a 1:1 reflection of source tables. No joins between models in this layer.</p><p><strong>Transformations — </strong>Light modifications like data type casting, column renaming, and (optional) filtering out deleted records.</p><p><strong>Purpose — </strong>To cleanse, standardize, and prepare raw data for downstream consumption.</p><h3><strong>Intermediate Layer</strong></h3><p><strong>Models — </strong>Intermediate models (<code>int_*</code>): Apply business logic to prepare data for fact modeling.</p><p><strong>Structure — </strong>These models build upon the clean and transformed data from the staging layer, focusing on <strong>business logic and calculations</strong> relevant to specific use cases that can feed into facts.</p><p><strong>Transformations — </strong>Heavier transformations compared to staging, including aggregations, calculations, and applying business logic. Uses CASE statements, window functions, and other advanced SQL techniques.</p><p><strong>Purpose — </strong>The intermediate layer acts as a bridge between the raw data sources (loaded in the staging area) and the final consumable data models (marts)</p><h3><strong>Marts Layer</strong></h3><p><strong>Models</strong></p><ul><li>Dimension models (<code>dim_*</code>): Represent slowly changing entities (e.g., customers, products).</li><li>Fact models (<code>fact_*</code>): Contain quantitative measures associated with dimensions.</li></ul><p><strong>Structure — </strong>Utilizes joins to combine data from various facts, dims and intermediate modes. Can involve unions for combining similar fact data.</p><p><strong>Transformations — </strong>No Transformation logic reside in marts layer</p><p><strong>Purpose —</strong>To transform data into a dimensional model that reflects the business’s perspective and supports analytics.</p><img src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*zntx0x2taPSRaeRXw6TQsA.png" alt="" /><h3><strong>Model Contracts</strong></h3><p>For model contracts refer to the below article</p><p><a href="https://medium.com/@emp0-data-whisperer/dbt-model-contracts-e0a6a6e58c0e" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">https://medium.com/@emp0-data-whisperer/dbt-model-contracts-e0a6a6e58c0e</a></p>
