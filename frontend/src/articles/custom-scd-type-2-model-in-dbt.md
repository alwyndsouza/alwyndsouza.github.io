<p><em>Building robust data warehouses that preserve historical context</em></p><p>Building a custom SCD Type 2 model in dbt is a powerful way to track historical changes without relying solely on the built-in <code>snapshot</code> functionality, giving you more control over the logic. While dbt snapshots are a great, simple solution for many use cases, a custom approach is often necessary when handling more complex scenarios, such as specific merge strategies, backfilling historical data, or dealing with source systems that lack a reliable <code>updated_at</code> column.</p><p>The core of the custom logic involves a few key steps within a dbt model materialized as an <code>incremental</code> table.</p><h3>Step-by-Step Logic for Custom SCD Type 2</h3><p>Your dbt model will use the <code>incremental</code> materialization, which allows dbt to smartly update and insert records based on your logic, rather than rebuilding the entire table every time. Here&#39;s a breakdown of the SQL logic you&#39;d write:</p><ol><li><strong>Identify New and Changed Records:</strong> You’ll start by selecting all records from your source table (e.g., <code>stg_customers</code>). A common and robust way to detect changes is to create a hash of the columns you want to track. The <code>dbt-utils</code> package has a handy <code>surrogate_key</code> macro for this. If the hash of a new record is different from the hash of the latest version of that record in your dimension table, you know a change has occurred.</li><li><strong>Expire Old Records:</strong> This is the most critical part of the SCD Type 2 logic. For every record in your source data that has a new, different version (detected in the previous step), you need to “close out” the old, currently active record in your dimension table. You do this by setting its <code>end_date</code> to the timestamp of the new record&#39;s arrival and changing its <code>active_flag</code> from <code>Y</code> to <code>N</code>.</li><li><strong>Insert New Records:</strong> For every new or changed record from your source data, you’ll insert a new row into your dimension table. This new row will have the updated information, a <code>start_date</code> set to the current timestamp, and the <code>end_date</code> set to a future value (like &#39;9999-12-31&#39;) to indicate it&#39;s the current, active version. Its <code>active_flag</code> will be <code>Y</code>.</li></ol><p>This process ensures that your dimension table is always up-to-date with the latest information, while also preserving a full, historical timeline of every change. The <code>dbt-utils</code> package and <code>incremental</code> materialization make writing this custom logic much simpler than writing raw SQL.</p><p>Let’s illustrate this with a simple example:</p><h3>Before Change:</h3><pre spellcheck="false">customer_id | name | city | active_flag | start_date | end_date<br />1001 | John Smith | New York | Y | 2023-01-01 | 9999-12-31</pre><h3>After John moves to Boston:</h3><pre spellcheck="false">customer_id | name | city | active_flag | start_date | end_date<br />1001 | John Smith | New York | N | 2023-01-01 | 2023-06-15<br />1001 | John Smith | Boston | Y | 2023-06-15 | 9999-12-31</pre><h3>dbt Implementation Architecture</h3><p>Our SCD Type 2 implementation follows a three-layer architecture:</p><pre spellcheck="false">Source System (CRM) → Staging Layer → Intermediate Layer → Mart Layer</pre><h3>1. The Staging Layer</h3><p>First, we extract raw customer data from TechMart’s CRM system:</p><pre spellcheck="false">-- models/staging/stg_crm__customers.sql<br />select<br /> customer_id,<br /> row_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> last_modified_date,<br /> operation_type -- INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE<br />from {{ source(&#x27;crm&#x27;, &#x27;customers&#x27;) }}</pre><h3>2. The Intermediate Layer</h3><p>Here we add SCD-specific logic and generate unique identifiers:</p><pre spellcheck="false">-- models/intermediate/int_crm__customers_scd.sql<br />select<br /> *,<br /> {{ dbt.generate_surrogate_key([<br /> &#x27;customer_id&#x27;, <br /> &#x27;customer_name&#x27;, <br /> &#x27;email_address&#x27;,<br /> &#x27;phone_number&#x27;,<br /> &#x27;city&#x27;,<br /> &#x27;marketing_consent_email&#x27;<br /> ]) }} as scd_id,<br /> coalesce(last_modified_date, current_timestamp) as effective_start_date<br />from {{ ref(&#x27;stg_crm__customers&#x27;) }}</pre><h3>3. The Mart Layer — The SCD Type 2 Magic</h3><p>Now comes the complex part — the actual SCD Type 2 implementation:</p><pre spellcheck="false">-- models/marts/dim_customers.sql<br />{{<br /> config(<br /> materialized=&#x27;incremental&#x27;,<br /> unique_key=&#x27;scd_id&#x27;,<br /> incremental_strategy=&#x27;delete+insert&#x27;<br /> )<br />}}<br /><br />with source_rows as (<br /> select<br /> customer_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> effective_start_date,<br /> null as effective_end_date,<br /> scd_id<br /> from {{ ref(&#x27;int_crm__customers_scd&#x27;) }}<br />)<br /><br />{% if is_incremental() %}<br /> , destination_rows as (<br /> select<br /> customer_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> effective_start_date,<br /> effective_end_date,<br /> scd_id<br /> from {{ this }} <br /> where active_flag = &#x27;Y&#x27;<br /> )<br /><br /> , new_valid_to as (<br /> select<br /> d.customer_id,<br /> s.effective_start_date as effective_end_date<br /> from source_rows s<br /> join destination_rows d<br /> on s.customer_id = d.customer_id<br /> and s.scd_id != d.scd_id<br /><br /> )<br /><br /> , add_new_valid_to as (<br /> select<br /> d.customer_id,<br /> d.customer_name,<br /> d.first_name,<br /> d.last_name,<br /> d.email_address,<br /> d.phone_number,<br /> d.annual_income,<br /> d.city,<br /> d.marketing_consent_email,<br /> d.marketing_consent_sms,<br /> d.contact_preference,<br /> d.effective_start_date,<br /> n.effective_end_date,<br /> d.scd_id,<br /> case when n.effective_end_date is null then &#x27;Y&#x27; else &#x27;N&#x27; end as active_flag<br /> from destination_rows d<br /> left join new_valid_to n<br /> on d.customer_id = n.customer_id<br /><br /> )<br /><br /> select<br /> customer_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> effective_start_date,<br /> effective_end_date,<br /> active_flag,<br /> scd_id<br /> from add_new_valid_to n<br /> union<br />{% endif %}<br /><br />select<br /> s.customer_id,<br /> s.customer_name,<br /> s.first_name,<br /> s.last_name,<br /> s.email_address,<br /> s.phone_number,<br /> s.annual_income,<br /> s.city,<br /> s.marketing_consent_email,<br /> s.marketing_consent_sms,<br /> s.contact_preference,<br /> s.effective_start_date,<br /> coalesce(<br /> lead(s.effective_start_date, 1) over (<br /> partition by s.customer_id<br /> order by s.effective_start_date<br /> ),<br /> &#x27;9999-12-31&#x27;::timestamp<br /> ) as effective_end_date,<br /> case<br /> when lead(s.effective_start_date, 1) over (<br /> partition by s.customer_id<br /> order by s.effective_start_date<br /> ) is null then &#x27;Y&#x27;<br /> else &#x27;N&#x27;<br /> end as active_flag,<br /> s.scd_id<br />from source_rows s</pre><h3>Breaking Down the Complex Logic</h3><p>Let’s examine the key components:</p><h3>1. Window Functions for Temporal Logic</h3><p>The heart of SCD Type 2 lies in understanding temporal relationships:</p><pre spellcheck="false">lead(effective_start_date) over (<br /> partition by customer_id <br /> order by effective_start_date<br />)</pre><p>This window function looks at the <strong>next</strong> record for the same customer. If there’s no next record, this is the current active version.</p><h3>2. Active Flag Determination</h3><pre spellcheck="false">case<br /> when lead(effective_start_date) over (...) is null then &#x27;Y&#x27;<br /> else &#x27;N&#x27;<br />end as active_flag</pre><p>Only the latest record for each customer gets an active flag of ‘Y’.</p><h3>3. Effective End Date Calculation</h3><pre spellcheck="false">coalesce(<br /> lead(effective_start_date) over (...), <br /> &#x27;9999-12-31&#x27;<br />) as effective_end_date</pre><p>The end date is either the start date of the next record, or our “forever” date (9999–12–31).</p><h3>Incremental Processing</h3><p>The real complexity emerges in incremental runs. We need to:</p><ol><li><strong>Identify</strong> existing active records that need to be closed</li><li><strong>Update</strong> their end dates and active flags</li><li><strong>Insert</strong> new records from the source</li></ol><pre spellcheck="false">{% if is_incremental() %}<br /> , destination_rows as (<br /> select<br /> customer_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> effective_start_date,<br /> effective_end_date,<br /> scd_id<br /> from {{ this }} <br /> where active_flag = &#x27;Y&#x27;<br /> )<br /><br /> , new_valid_to as (<br /> select<br /> d.customer_id,<br /> s.effective_start_date as effective_end_date<br /> from source_rows s<br /> join destination_rows d<br /> on s.customer_id = d.customer_id<br /> and s.scd_id != d.scd_id<br /> )<br /><br /> , add_new_valid_to as (<br /> select<br /> d.customer_id,<br /> d.customer_name,<br /> d.first_name,<br /> d.last_name,<br /> d.email_address,<br /> d.phone_number,<br /> d.annual_income,<br /> d.city,<br /> d.marketing_consent_email,<br /> d.marketing_consent_sms,<br /> d.contact_preference,<br /> d.effective_start_date,<br /> n.effective_end_date,<br /> d.scd_id,<br /> case when n.effective_end_date is null then &#x27;Y&#x27; else &#x27;N&#x27; end as active_flag<br /> from destination_rows d<br /> left join new_valid_to n<br /> on d.customer_id = n.customer_id<br /> )<br /><br /> select<br /> customer_id,<br /> customer_name,<br /> first_name,<br /> last_name,<br /> email_address,<br /> phone_number,<br /> annual_income,<br /> city,<br /> marketing_consent_email,<br /> marketing_consent_sms,<br /> contact_preference,<br /> effective_start_date,<br /> effective_end_date,<br /> active_flag,<br /> scd_id<br /> from add_new_valid_to n<br /> union<br />{% endif %}</pre><p>Here’s a brief breakdown of each part:</p><ul><li><code><strong>destination_rows</strong></code>: This section queries the existing dimension table (<code>{{ this }}</code>) to find all currently <strong>active records</strong> (<code>where active_flag = &#39;Y&#39;</code>). It&#39;s the &quot;before&quot; snapshot of the most recent data.</li><li><code><strong>new_valid_to</strong></code>: This is the core logic for expiring old records. It performs a <code>JOIN</code> between the new incoming data (<code>source_rows</code>) and the current active records (<code>destination_rows</code>). It identifies where a <code>customer_id</code> matches but the <code>scd_id</code> (the hash of the record) is different. This tells the model, &quot;A new version of this customer record exists, so the old one needs to be ended.&quot; It then sets the <code>effective_end_date</code> for the old record to be the <code>effective_start_date</code> of the new one.</li><li><code><strong>add_new_valid_to</strong></code>: This part takes all of the currently active records (<code>destination_rows</code>) and &quot;adds&quot; the new <code>effective_end_date</code> to them, based on the results from <code>new_valid_to</code>. It also updates the <code>active_flag</code> to <code>&#39;N&#39;</code> for any records that are about to be expired.</li><li><code><strong>union</strong></code>: The final <code>select</code> statement combines two datasets: the records from <code>add_new_valid_to</code> (which now includes both expired and still-active records) and the brand new records from <code>source_rows</code>. This union creates the complete result set that dbt will use to <code>delete+insert</code> and replace the data in the final table. This ensures the historical integrity is maintained by correctly closing out old records and adding new ones.</li></ul><h3>Real-World Example Walkthrough</h3><p>Let’s trace through TechMart’s customer John Smith:</p><p><strong>Day 1 — Initial Load:</strong></p><pre spellcheck="false">customer_id | name | email | city | active | start_date | end_date<br />1001 | John Smith | john@email.com | New York | Y | 2023-01-01 | 9999-12-31</pre><p><strong>Day 30 — Email Change:</strong></p><pre spellcheck="false">customer_id | name | email | city | active | start_date | end_date<br />1001 | John Smith | john@email.com | New York | N | 2023-01-01 | 2023-01-30<br />1001 | John Smith | j.smith@new.com | New York | Y | 2023-01-30 | 9999-12-31</pre><p><strong>Day 60 — Moves to Boston:</strong></p><pre spellcheck="false">customer_id | name | email | city | active | start_date | end_date<br />1001 | John Smith | john@email.com | New York | N | 2023-01-01 | 2023-01-30<br />1001 | John Smith | j.smith@new.com | New York | N | 2023-01-30 | 2023-03-01<br />1001 | John Smith | j.smith@new.com | Boston | Y | 2023-03-01 | 9999-12-31</pre><h3>References</h3><p><a href="https://gist.github.com/jeremyyeo/3a23f3fbcb72f10a17fc4d31b8a47854" rel="nofollow noopener" target="_blank">https://gist.github.com/jeremyyeo/3a23f3fbcb72f10a17fc4d31b8a47854</a></p>
